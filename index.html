2Undershoot and overshoot

The endpoint is the first colour change that stays after swirling. It should be within one drop of the equivalence point, where the reactants are in exactly the mole ratio of the equation.

1 mL earlyequivalence1 mL late

3The rule: the answer follows the titre

The titrant is in the burette and the unknown is in the flask. Every step of the calculation multiplies or divides by numbers that don’t change, so whatever happens to the titre happens to the answer.

Check the titrant first. If its real concentration isn’t what the label says (the burette was rinsed with water, say), a different volume is needed. The calculation still uses the label, so work out the titre, then apply the rule.
Unknown in the burette? Then the rule flips. The unknown’s concentration is n ÷ titre, so a bigger titre gives a smaller answer.

4Rinsing errors

The final rinse is with the solution the glassware will hold or deliver. The conical flask is the exception: deionised water is fine. Click any cell to see why.

5Other common errors

Ask the class first: does the titre go up, down or stay the same? Then the answer follows the titre (step 3).

Systematic or one-off? Rinsing errors, an unsuitable indicator or a titrant that doesn’t match its label push every trial the same way. Concordant titres won’t reveal them and averaging won’t fix them. An overshoot in one trial is a one-off: that titre won’t be concordant, so the trial is repeated and left out of the average.
